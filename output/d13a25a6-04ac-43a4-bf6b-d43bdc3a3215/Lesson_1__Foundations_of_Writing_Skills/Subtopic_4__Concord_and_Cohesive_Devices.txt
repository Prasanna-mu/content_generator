#### Concord and Cohesive Devices

**What are Concord and Cohesive Devices?**
Concord involves using words and phrases that agree in number, gender, and case. Cohesive devices, on the other hand, are mechanisms that connect ideas within a sentence or paragraph to create a cohesive and smooth flow. Examples include coordinating and subordinating conjunctions, pronouns, and other linking words.

**Types of Concord:**
1. **Number Concord:** Ensures that verbs and nouns agree in their number. For example, using "both" requires "both" to be preceded by a plural verb, such as "both are" or "both were."
2. **Gender Concord:** Ensures that nouns and pronouns agree in gender. For example, using "he" requires a singular masculine pronoun, like "he."
3. **Case Concord:** Ensures that nouns, pronouns, and adjectives agree in their grammatical case. For example, using "its" instead of "it's" ensures the correct case.

**Types of Cohesive Devices:**
1. **Coordinating Conjunctions:** Used to join words, phrases, and clauses. Examples include "and," "but," and "or." For instance, "She likes apples, and she likes oranges" uses "and" to connect two related ideas.
2. **Subordinating Conjunctions:** Used to introduce a subordinate clause. Examples include "although," "because," and "since." For example, "Although she loves apples, she doesn't like oranges" uses "Although" to introduce a clause.
3. **Pronouns:** Used to replace nouns and can connect ideas. Examples include "I," "you," "he," "she," "it," "we," "they," and personal pronouns like "my," "your," "his," "her," "our," "their," and possessive pronouns like "mine," "yours," "his," "hers," "ours," and "theirs."
4. **Prepositions:** Used to connect nouns, pronouns, and phrases to other words. For example, "in" or "on" to introduce a location.
5. **Signpost Words:** Used to signal the direction of the text, such as "however," "therefore," "in conclusion," and "on the other hand."
