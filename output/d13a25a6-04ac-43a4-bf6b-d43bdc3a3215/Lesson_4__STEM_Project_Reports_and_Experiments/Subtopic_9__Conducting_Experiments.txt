### Subtopic 9: Conducting Experiments

#### Lesson 4: STEM Project Reports and Experiments

In this lesson, we'll cover how to design and conduct experiments as part of a project report. Understanding how to properly design and execute experiments is crucial in STEM fields, ensuring your project findings are reliable and valid.

**Designing Experiments:**
1. **Hypothesis Development:** Start with a clear hypothesis that you can test through your experiment.
2. **Controlled Variables:** Identify and control all variables except the one being tested.
3. **Replication:** Ensure your experiment can be replicated by others to validate results.
4. **Replication and Reproducibility:** Repeat the experiment multiple times and ensure the results are consistent.

**Conducting Experiments:**
1. **Materials and Equipment:** List all materials and equipment needed for the experiment.
2. **Procedure:** Write a detailed step-by-step procedure for your experiment.
3. **Data Collection:** Record all data collected during the experiment accurately.
4. **Data Analysis:** Use appropriate statistical methods to analyze the data collected.

**Example Experiment:**
Let's say you're conducting an experiment to test the effect of different fertilizers on plant growth. Your hypothesis could be: "Fertilizer A will result in higher plant growth compared to Fertilizer B."

- **Materials:** Plants, Fertilizer A, Fertilizer B, soil, water, measuring cups, rulers.
- **Procedure:** 
  1. Plant 10 seeds in each of two pots.
  2. Water the plants as needed.
  3. Apply Fertilizer A to one pot and Fertilizer B to the other.
  4. Measure and record plant heights after 30 days.
- **Data Collection:** Record the height of each plant, the amount of fertilizer used, and the water given.
- **Data Analysis:** Use a t-test to compare the mean heights of plants between the two groups.

**Writing Experiments in Reports:**
In your project report, describe the experiment in detail, including the materials, procedures, and analysis. Use clear, concise language and include any statistical data.

**Example Report:**
"In this experiment, we tested the effect of two different fertilizers on plant growth. Each of 10 seeds was planted in a pot and given either Fertilizer A or Fertilizer B. The plants were watered regularly and their heights measured after 30 days. A t-test showed that plants treated with Fertilizer A grew significantly taller than those treated with Fertilizer B."

Understanding how to design and conduct experiments is fundamental in STEM projects. It helps ensure your project findings are robust and reliable, making your report credible and valuable.
