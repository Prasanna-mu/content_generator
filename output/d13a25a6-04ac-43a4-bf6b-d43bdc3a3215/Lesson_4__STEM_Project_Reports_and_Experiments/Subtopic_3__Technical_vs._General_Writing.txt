### Subtopic: Technical vs. General Writing

#### Lesson: Technical vs. General Writing

In STEM project reports and experiments, you'll often find yourself writing in both technical and general contexts. Understanding the differences is crucial for effective communication. Here’s a quick guide:

**Technical Writing:**
- **Purpose:** Detailed, specialized content for experts in the field.
- **Audience:** Colleagues, industry professionals, experts.
- **Structure:** Clear, concise, and detailed. Uses technical terms and jargon.
- **Examples:** Code snippets, detailed explanations, diagrams.

**General Writing:**
- **Purpose:** Broad, accessible content for a general audience.
- **Audience:** Students, general readers, non-specialists.
- **Structure:** More conversational, uses simpler language, less technical terms.
- **Examples:** Introduction, background, discussion.

**Key Differences:**
- **Jargon:** Technical uses specialized terms, general avoids.
- **Clarity:** Technical is precise, general is clear and accessible.
- **Examples:** A technical report might describe a complex algorithm, a general write-up could explain the algorithm's basics.

**Concordance in STEM:**
- **Technical Writing:** Uses terms like "API," "API gateway," "microservice."
- **General Writing:** Uses "web service," "interface," "service layer."

**Signpost Words:**
- **Technical:** "First, second, then, finally," "Subsequently, concurrently."
- **General:** "In the beginning, next, then, lastly."

**Cohesive Devices:**
- **Technical:** "Hence," "Therefore," "As a result."
- **General:** "So," "And," "Then," "Finally."

**Example Paragraph:**
- **Technical:** "The microservice architecture is a design pattern where applications are built as a suite of small services, each running its own process."
- **General:** "Microservices are small, independent programs that work together to create a larger application."

Understanding these differences will help you tailor your writing to the right audience and purpose, making your STEM project reports and experiments more effective and engaging.
