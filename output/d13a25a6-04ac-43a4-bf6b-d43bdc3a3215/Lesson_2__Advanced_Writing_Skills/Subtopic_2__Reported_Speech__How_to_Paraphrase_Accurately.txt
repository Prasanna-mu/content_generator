### Lesson 2: Advanced Writing Skills

#### Subtopic 2: Accurate Reported Speech: Paraphrasing Techniques

**Objective:** Master the art of reporting speech accurately. Understand the difference between direct and indirect reported speech.

**Direct Reported Speech:**
Direct reported speech is when you repeat someone's exact words. For example:

- Direct: "She said, 'I will be in the meeting.'"
- Indirect: "She said that she would be in the meeting."

**Indirect Reported Speech:**
Indirect reported speech involves paraphrasing someone's speech. It uses the past tense and a connecting verb (usually "said") and a comma to set off the reported speech. Here are examples:

- Direct: "She said, 'I am going to the store.'"
- Indirect: "She said that she was going to the store."

**Mastering Accurate Paraphrasing:**

1. **Substitution:**
   Instead of using "she said," you can use "she remarked," "she announced," or "she informed."

   - Direct: "She said, 'I am going to the store.'"
   - Indirect: "She remarked that she was going to the store."

2. **Omission of "said":**
   In indirect speech, you can omit "said" and still maintain clarity. This is especially useful when the quote is short.

   - Direct: "She said, 'I am going to the store.'"
   - Indirect: "She said I was going to the store."

3. **Use of "that":**
   The indirect speech uses "that" to introduce the reported speech. This is especially important when the reported speech is more than just a quote.

   - Direct: "She said, 'I will be in the meeting.'"
   - Indirect: "She said that she would be in the meeting."

**Example of Accurate Paraphrasing:**
- Direct: "She said, 'I am going to the store.'"
- Indirect: "She said that she would be going to the store."

**Common Mistakes to Avoid:**

1. **Incorrect Tense:**
   Ensure you use the correct tense. Direct speech might use the present tense, while indirect speech uses the past tense.

2. **Omission of "said":**
   Always include "said" in indirect speech, even if the quote is short. It helps maintain clarity and clarity.

3. **Incorrect Use of "That":**
   Use "that" correctly to introduce the reported speech. It's crucial for clarity and readability.

By mastering these techniques, you can accurately paraphrase speech in reports, making your writing clear, concise, and professional.
