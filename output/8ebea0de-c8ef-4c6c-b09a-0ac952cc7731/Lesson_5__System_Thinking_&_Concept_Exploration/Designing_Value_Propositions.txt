### Subtopic: Exploring Value Propositions

#### Lesson: Value Proposition Design

Designing value propositions is a critical part of applied design thinking, especially when aiming to understand and address customer needs effectively. This lesson focuses on how to conduct customer interviews and field visits, and how to develop a compelling value proposition using Minimum Usable Prototypes (MUPs).

#### Overview

Value propositions are essential for clearly communicating the value that your product or service offers to customers. They articulate the unique benefits that differentiate your offering from competitors. This lesson will cover the importance of customer-centric innovation, problem validation, and customer discovery. It will also introduce the Forge 231 Ovation Rubric, a tool to mitigate validation risk.

#### Importance of Customer-Centric Innovation

Customer-centric innovation means designing solutions that are deeply rooted in understanding customer needs and pain points. It involves problem validation, which is the process of confirming whether a specific problem exists and is significant enough to be addressed. This is followed by customer discovery, where you learn more about your customers' needs and preferences.

#### Concept of Minimum Usable Prototype (MUP)

An MUP is a simplified version of your product or service that allows you to gather customer feedback and validate your value proposition. It’s a practical way to test and refine your concept without requiring extensive development.

**Example:**
```java
// MUP Challenge Brief
public MUPChallengeBrief generateMUPChallengeBrief(String valueProposition) {
    // Logic to generate MUP challenge brief
    return new MUPChallengeBrief(valueProposition);
}
```
